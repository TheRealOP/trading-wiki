# Moving Average Crossover Strategies

## Key Insight
[[Moving average]] crossover strategies are a foundational [[trend-following]] technique. They generate signals by comparing a shorter-period average to a longer-period average to identify trend direction. While susceptible to [[whipsaw]]s in ranging markets, they can provide a systematic way to capture gains during sustained trends, often outperforming buy-and-hold on a [[risk-adjusted return]] basis.

## The Math
The core component is the [[moving average]]. The two most common types are:

1.  **Simple Moving Average ([[SMA]])**: The unweighted mean of the previous N prices.
    $SMA_N = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} P_{t-i}$
    Where $P_t$ is the price at time $t$ and $N$ is the lookback window.

2.  **Exponential Moving Average ([[EMA]])**: A weighted average that gives more significance to recent prices.
    $EMA_t = (P_t \times k) + EMA_{t-1} \times (1 - k)$
    Where the smoothing factor $k = \frac{2}{N+1}$.

## Strategy Logic
A common implementation is the [[golden cross]] / [[death cross]] strategy:
1.  Calculate a short-period [[moving average]] (e.g., [[SMA(50)]]).
2.  Calculate a long-period [[moving average]] (e.g., [[SMA(200)]]).
3.  **Entry Signal**: When the short-period MA crosses above the long-period MA, [[buy]]. This is the [[golden cross]].
4.  **Exit Signal**: When the short-period MA crosses below the long-period MA, [[sell]]. This is the [[death cross]].

## Parameters
- **Short MA Period**: Lookback window for the fast moving average.
- **Long MA Period**: Lookback window for the slow moving average.
- **Asset Universe**: Which tickers to apply the strategy to (e.g., [[SPY]], [[BTC-USD]]).
- **Timeframe**: The frequency of the price data (e.g., daily, hourly).

## Risks
- **[[Lag]]**: As lagging indicators, MAs will always be late in identifying a trend change.
- **[[Whipsaw]]**: In non-trending, sideways markets, the strategy can generate many false signals, leading to frequent small losses that erode capital.
- **[[Parameter risk]]**: The strategy's performance is highly sensitive to the chosen MA periods, which may not be robust across different [[market regime]]s.
